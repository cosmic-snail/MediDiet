# Gout Dietary Management Guidelines

Source: ACR 2020 Guideline for the Management of Gout

## Purine Restriction

Patients with gout should limit intake of high-purine foods. High-purine foods
to avoid: organ meats (liver, kidney, sweetbreads), red meat (beef, lamb, pork
in excess), certain seafood (anchovies, sardines, mackerel, mussels, scallops),
and gravy or meat-based broths. Moderate purine foods (limit to 1-2 servings
per day): poultry, fish, legumes, mushrooms, spinach, asparagus, cauliflower.

## Alcohol Avoidance

Alcohol consumption, especially beer and liquor, should be avoided during acute
gout flares. Beer is particularly problematic due to its high purine content
from brewer's yeast. Wine in moderation (1 glass per day) may be acceptable for
some patients during inter-critical periods.

## Fructose Limitation

Limit sugar-sweetened beverages and foods high in fructose. Fructose increases
uric acid production. Avoid: soft drinks, fruit juices with added sugar, high-
fructose corn syrup products, and excessive consumption of sweet fruits.

## Hydration

Maintain adequate hydration: at least 2 liters (2000 mL) of water per day.
Adequate hydration helps promote uric acid excretion through the kidneys.
Water is the preferred fluid; avoid dehydration.

## Dairy and Plant Protein

Low-fat dairy products may have a protective effect and can help lower serum
uric acid levels. Plant-based protein sources (legumes, tofu) are preferred
over animal proteins. Cherry consumption (fresh cherries or cherry extract)
may help reduce gout flare frequency.

## Weight Management

Achieve and maintain a healthy body weight. Weight loss should be gradual
(0.5-1 kg per week) as rapid weight loss can trigger gout flares through
ketosis and increased uric acid levels. Obesity is a major risk factor for
hyperuricemia.
